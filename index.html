🔧 Professional Shoe Repair Guide

Complete reference for shoe repair professionals

💡 Tip of the Day:

⚡ Emergency Quick Fixes

These are temporary solutions for urgent situations. Customer should return for proper repair.

🚨 Sole Detaching - Immediate Fix

Easy ⏱️ 5 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Customer needs shoes NOW - wedding, interview, important meeting.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Super glue or Shoe Goo
  • Clamps or rubber bands
  • Paper towels
  • Quick-dry spray (optional)
1 Clean quickly: Wipe separation area with dry paper towel. Remove visible dirt only.
2 Apply glue: Apply super glue or Shoe Goo directly into separation. Work fast.
3 Press firmly: Press sole and upper together firmly for 30-60 seconds.
4 Secure with bands: Wrap rubber bands around shoe to maintain pressure. Leave for 3-5 minutes minimum.
5 Quick set: Use accelerator spray if available, or hairdryer on cool setting to speed bonding.
💡 Tell Customer: "This will hold for today, but please bring them back tomorrow for proper repair. Don't get them wet if possible."

🚨 Broken Heel - Walking Emergency

Easy ⏱️ 3 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Customer can't walk properly. Heel completely broken off or hanging.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Strong tape (duct tape or gaffer tape)
  • Scissors
  • OR: Remove heel completely and flatten shoe
1 Option A - Tape it: If heel is hanging, push it back into position and wrap multiple layers of strong tape around heel and shoe. Make it tight.
2 Option B - Remove it: If heel is too damaged, completely remove it and any sharp parts. Customer can walk flat but safely.
3 Match the pair: If one heel breaks, offer to remove the other heel too so customer can walk evenly.
💡 Tell Customer: "This is just to get you through today. Come back tomorrow and we'll do a proper heel replacement."

🚨 Strap/Buckle Broken - Can't Wear Shoe

Easy ⏱️ 5 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Strap broken, buckle snapped - shoe won't stay on foot.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Safety pins or paper clips
  • Strong thread and needle
  • OR: Velcro strips (self-adhesive)
  • Pliers
1 Safety pin method: Use large safety pin to reconnect strap to shoe body. Pin from inside if possible to hide it.
2 Quick stitch: If you have 2 minutes more, do 4-5 quick hand stitches through strap and shoe. Doesn't need to be pretty.
3 Velcro backup: Stick adhesive Velcro strip to strap and shoe for temporary closure.
4 Paper clip hack: Bend paper clip into S-shape to reconnect broken buckle parts temporarily.
💡 Tell Customer: "This will hold the shoe on your foot today. Schedule a proper strap replacement this week."

🚨 Lace Broke - No Replacement Available

Easy ⏱️ 2 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Lace snapped, no replacement laces in stock or customer can't wait.
1 Tie broken ends: If lace broke in middle, tie the two pieces together with small tight knot. Tuck knot inside shoe.
2 Skip lacing: If too short now, skip some middle eyelets and lace only top and bottom for temporary hold.
3 String alternative: In extreme emergency, use strong string, paracord, or even ribbon as temporary lace.
4 One shoe solution: If they have two shoes, steal the lace from the other shoe and lace both shoes partially.
💡 Tell Customer: "This will work for today. Come back anytime and we'll put proper laces in for you."

🚨 Zipper Stuck/Broken - Can't Get Shoe On

Easy ⏱️ 3 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Zipper won't move or pull came off. Customer needs to wear boots NOW.
1 Zipper stuck: Rub graphite pencil along zipper teeth or apply bar soap. Work zipper gently back and forth.
2 Pull broke off: Use paper clip or small safety pin through the slider hole as temporary pull.
3 Zipper won't close: Use pliers to gently squeeze slider sides closer together. Often fixes loose sliders.
4 Complete failure: Safety pin or stitch the zipper closed in functional position. Customer can slip shoe on/off without unzipping.
💡 Tell Customer: "This should help you through today. If zipper needs replacement, bring them back when you have time."

🚨 Insole Coming Out - Foot Sliding Around

Easy ⏱️ 2 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Insole is loose, bunching up, or sliding around making shoe unwearable.
1 Quick glue: Lift insole, apply dots of super glue or shoe glue at heel and toe areas only.
2 Press and hold: Press insole down firmly for 30 seconds. Let set for 2 minutes.
3 Double-sided tape: If no glue, use carpet tape or strong double-sided tape at heel and arch.
4 Remove it: If insole is damaged, just remove it completely. Better than having it bunch up.
💡 Tell Customer: "This quick fix will hold for a few days. If you need a new insole, we can replace it properly later."

🚨 Huge Hole in Sole - Can't Walk in Rain

Easy ⏱️ 5 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Large hole in sole, customer's foot getting wet, needs immediate waterproofing.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Shoe Goo or flexible waterproof sealant
  • Duct tape or Gorilla tape
  • Cardboard or thick leather scrap
1 Patch from inside: Cut cardboard or leather piece slightly larger than hole. Place inside shoe over hole.
2 Fill with Shoe Goo: From outside, fill hole completely with Shoe Goo. Spread smooth.
3 Tape the outside: Cover repair with waterproof tape on bottom of sole. Press firmly.
4 Let set: Wait 5 minutes minimum before walking. Avoid puddles for first day.
💡 Tell Customer: "This emergency patch will keep your foot dry today. Come back soon for a proper sole repair or replacement."

🚨 Torn Leather - Visible Ugly Rip

Easy ⏱️ 4 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Visible tear in leather upper, customer has important event and tear is very noticeable.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Leather glue or super glue
  • Matching shoe polish or marker
  • Small piece of thin leather (for backing)
  • Clamps or tape
1 Glue from inside: If you can access inside of shoe, place small leather piece behind tear as backing patch.
2 Align edges: Apply tiny amount of glue to tear edges. Press together carefully to align as perfectly as possible.
3 Hold pressure: Use small clamps or tape over tear (on outside) to hold while setting. Wait 2-3 minutes.
4 Color match: Once dry, use matching polish or leather marker to blend the tear line. Buff gently.
💡 Tell Customer: "This will hide the tear for your event today. Bring them back for proper leather repair with stitching."

🚨 Loud Squeaking - In Meeting/Church

Easy ⏱️ 1 minute
URGENT SITUATION: Shoes squeaking embarrassingly loud. Customer desperate for immediate silence.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Baby powder or cornstarch
  • OR: Bar of soap
  • OR: WD-40 (use sparingly)
1 Powder method: Sprinkle baby powder generously under insole and around inside edges. Shake shoe around.
2 Soap on sole: If squeak is from sole, rub bar soap on bottom of shoe. This stops rubber squeaking on floors.
3 Quick spray: For leather-on-leather squeak, tiny spray of WD-40 between rubbing parts. Wipe excess immediately.
💡 Tell Customer: "This will stop the squeaking for today. If it comes back, bring them in and we'll find the real source."

🚨 Shoes Too Tight - Customer in Pain

Easy ⏱️ 3 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: New shoes pinching badly. Customer needs to wear them NOW and is in pain.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Shoe stretch spray OR rubbing alcohol in spray bottle
  • Thick socks
  • Hairdryer (if available)
1 Spray inside: Spray stretch solution or rubbing alcohol inside tight areas of shoe. Focus on pressure points.
2 Wear with thick socks: Have customer put on thick socks and squeeze feet into shoes immediately.
3 Heat and walk: If you have hairdryer, apply warm air to tight spots while customer walks around for 2-3 minutes.
4 Manual stretch: While shoes are damp, manually flex and bend the tight areas to loosen them up.
💡 Tell Customer: "This gives immediate relief. If still tight after today, bring them back for proper stretching on our machine."

🚨 Terrible Odor - Customer Embarrassed

Easy ⏱️ 2 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Shoes smell horrible. Customer has social event and is embarrassed.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Baking soda
  • Shoe deodorizer spray
  • Dryer sheets or tea bags
  • Rubbing alcohol
1 Spray inside: Spray shoe deodorizer or diluted rubbing alcohol (1:1 with water) inside entire shoe interior.
2 Baking soda blast: Pour generous amount of baking soda inside. Shake around vigorously. Pour out excess.
3 Insert freshener: Stuff shoes with dryer sheets or tea bags for customer to keep in overnight.
4 Quick air: If time allows, place in front of fan for 5-10 minutes while customer waits.
💡 Tell Customer: "This will help for tonight. For deep cleaning and lasting freshness, leave them with us for a full deodorizing treatment."

🚨 Slippery Soles - Customer Keeps Falling

Easy ⏱️ 3 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Brand new leather soles are dangerously slippery. Customer worried about falling.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Coarse sandpaper (60-80 grit)
  • OR: Utility knife
  • OR: Sole scoring tool
1 Sandpaper method: Rub coarse sandpaper vigorously across entire bottom of sole. Focus on heel and ball areas.
2 Scoring method: Use utility knife to make shallow crosshatch pattern across sole. Don't cut deep.
3 Test grip: Have customer test on smooth floor. Add more texture if needed.
4 Clean edges: Wipe away any loose material. Customer can wear immediately.
💡 Tell Customer: "This gives instant grip for safety. After a few wears, the sole will naturally roughen up more. If you want permanent grip, we can add rubber taps."

🚨 Elastic Gore Ripped Out

Easy ⏱️ 4 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Elastic side panel torn loose. Shoe won't stay on foot without it.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Large safety pins (3-4)
  • Heavy-duty thread and needle
  • OR: Fabric glue
1 Safety pin secure: Use 2-3 large safety pins to pin elastic back into position from inside of shoe.
2 Quick hand stitch: If time permits, do fast whip stitches along edge with heavy thread. 6-8 stitches minimum.
3 Glue backup: Apply fabric glue along pinned edge for extra hold. Don't rely on glue alone.
4 Test stretch: Have customer try on and test if elastic still stretches and holds properly.
💡 Tell Customer: "This emergency fix will get you through today. Schedule elastic panel replacement within the week for permanent repair."

🚨 Eyelet Ripped Out - Can't Lace Shoe

Easy ⏱️ 3 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Lace eyelet hole torn through. Can't lace properly and shoe won't tighten.
1 Skip the hole: Simply skip the damaged eyelet and lace around it. Adjust lacing pattern to compensate.
2 Create new hole: Use awl to punch new hole slightly above or below damaged one. Reinforce with super glue around edges.
3 Emergency grommet: If you have spare eyelets, quickly install one in new position with hand tool.
4 Tie off solution: Lace up to damaged eyelet, tie knot, then continue above it with remaining lace.
💡 Tell Customer: "This alternative lacing will work today. Come back and we'll install proper metal eyelets in the right positions."

🚨 White Shoes Badly Stained (Event Tonight)

Easy ⏱️ 5 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: White or light-colored shoes heavily stained. Customer has event tonight and shoes look terrible.

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Magic eraser sponge
  • White shoe cleaner or baking soda paste
  • Old toothbrush
  • White shoe polish or white-out pen
  • Paper towels
1 Magic eraser attack: Wet magic eraser and scrub stained areas vigorously. Works amazingly on rubber and leather.
2 Baking soda scrub: Make paste with baking soda and water. Scrub with toothbrush on stubborn stains. Wipe clean.
3 Cover remaining stains: Use white shoe polish or even white correction pen to cover any stains that won't come out.
4 Quick dry: Wipe with dry paper towels. Use hairdryer on cool if needed. Must be dry before customer wears.
💡 Tell Customer: "This gets them event-ready tonight. For deep professional cleaning and protection coating, bring them back when you have more time."

🚨 Sole Separated at Toe Only

Easy ⏱️ 4 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Just the toe area is separating and flapping. Makes walking difficult and looks bad.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Shoe Goo or super glue
  • Small clamps or binder clips
  • Toothpick or small brush
  • Rubber bands
1 Clean the gap: Use toothpick to remove dirt from separated area. Blow out debris.
2 Apply glue deeply: Use toothpick or small brush to work glue deep into separation. Get good coverage.
3 Press and clamp: Press toe area together firmly. Use small clamps or wrap rubber bands around toe to maintain pressure.
4 Set time: Must set for minimum 5 minutes. 10 minutes better. Remove clamps and customer can walk carefully.
💡 Tell Customer: "Avoid flexing the toe heavily today. This quick bond will hold for light walking. Bring back for full sole regluing when convenient."

🚨 Heel Tip Fell Off - Damaging Heel

Easy ⏱️ 2 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Small heel tip cap missing. Metal/plastic heel now clicking loudly and getting damaged.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Replacement heel tip (if in stock)
  • OR: Small rubber cap/furniture pad
  • OR: Several layers duct tape
  • Hammer (if installing proper tip)
1 Quick tip install: If you have matching heel tip, tap it onto heel post with hammer. Takes 30 seconds.
2 Rubber cap backup: If no heel tip available, push small rubber cap or furniture pad onto heel post. Wrap with tape if loose.
3 Tape method: As last resort, wrap heel end with several tight layers of duct tape. Trim neat with scissors.
💡 Tell Customer: "If this is a temporary cap, it'll last a few days. Come back and we'll put on a proper matching heel tip."

🚨 Buckle Won't Close - Pin Missing

Easy ⏱️ 2 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Buckle pin broke off or missing. Strap won't stay fastened.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Paper clip or safety pin
  • Small wire or twist tie
  • Pliers
  • OR: Velcro strip
1 Paper clip pin: Straighten paper clip. Thread through buckle frame where pin should be. Bend ends to secure.
2 Wire solution: Use small gauge wire. Thread through and twist tight with pliers. Trim sharp ends.
3 Velcro bypass: If buckle completely broken, add adhesive Velcro to strap and shoe body for temporary closure.
💡 Tell Customer: "This makeshift pin will hold today. We can replace the entire buckle properly when you return."

🚨 Shoelace Aglet Broke Off

Easy ⏱️ 1 minute
URGENT SITUATION: Plastic tip (aglet) fell off lace end. Can't thread lace through eyelets anymore.
1 Tape method: Wrap small piece of clear tape tightly around frayed lace end. Twist to point.
2 Super glue fix: Apply drop of super glue to lace end. Roll between fingers to form point while wet. Let dry 30 seconds.
3 Nail polish: Dip frayed end in clear nail polish. Let dry. Creates stiff pointed end.
4 Cut and singe: For synthetic laces, trim frayed end and quickly pass through lighter flame to melt/seal.
💡 Tell Customer: "This quick fix makes it laceable again. We have new laces in stock when you need them."

🚨 Metal Shank Poking Through Insole

Easy ⏱️ 3 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Sharp metal shank or nail poking up through insole. Painful and dangerous.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Hammer and pliers
  • Thick cardboard or leather piece
  • Strong glue
  • Extra insole or thick padding
1 Remove insole: Take out current insole to access the protruding point.
2 Hammer it down: Use hammer to carefully flatten the sharp point. Pull nail out with pliers if possible.
3 Add protective layer: Cut thick cardboard or leather to cover entire insole area. Glue down firmly.
4 Install thick insole: Place thick cushioned insole over protective layer. This adds barrier between foot and metal.
⚠️ Warning: If shank is badly damaged or broken, shoe structure is compromised. May need complete rebuild.
💡 Tell Customer: "This makes it safe to wear today. But bring them back soon - this indicates structural damage that needs proper repair."

🚨 Wet Shoes - Need Them Dry NOW

Easy ⏱️ 10 minutes
URGENT SITUATION: Shoes soaking wet from rain/spill. Customer needs to wear them within the hour.

🛠️ What You Need:

  • Newspaper or paper towels
  • Fan or hairdryer
  • Dry towels
  • Spare socks
1 Remove water: Shake out excess water. Wipe exterior thoroughly with dry towel.
2 Stuff with paper: Pack shoes tightly with crumpled newspaper or paper towels. This absorbs interior moisture fast.
3 Change paper: Replace paper every 2-3 minutes as it gets saturated. Do this 2-3 times.
4 Apply airflow: Place in front of strong fan or use hairdryer on COOL setting. Never use hot air on leather.
5 Backup plan: If still damp, offer spare socks to customer to wear inside as barrier. Better than wet feet.
⚠️ Warning: Never use direct heat on leather shoes - causes cracking. Always use cool air only.
💡 Tell Customer: "These are wearable but still slightly damp. Let them air dry fully tonight and condition the leather tomorrow to prevent damage."

Full Sole Replacement

Medium Difficulty ⏱️ 45-60 minutes Watch Video Tutorial

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Shoe last
  • Sole knife
  • Skiving knife
  • Contact cement
  • Replacement sole material
  • Hammer and nails (optional)
  • Sole press or clamps
1 Remove old sole: Use sole knife to carefully separate the old sole from the upper. Work slowly to avoid damaging the welt or upper material.
2 Clean the base: Remove all old adhesive and debris. Sand the surface lightly for better adhesion.
3 Prepare new sole: Trace and cut the new sole material to match the shoe's outline. Skive edges if needed.
4 Apply adhesive: Apply contact cement to both surfaces. Wait until tacky (5-10 minutes).
5 Attach and press: Carefully align and press the new sole. Use a sole press or heavy clamps for 24 hours.
6 Trim and finish: Trim excess material and sand edges smooth. Apply edge dressing if desired.
💡 Pro Tip: Always match the sole material to the shoe's intended use. Leather for dress shoes, rubber for casual/work boots.

Complete Heel Replacement

Hard ⏱️ 30-45 minutes Watch Video Tutorial

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Heel puller
  • Replacement heel block
  • Heel nails
  • Hammer
  • Rasp and sandpaper
  • Drill (if needed)
1 Remove old heel: Carefully pry off entire heel using heel puller. Watch for hidden nails or screws.
2 Assess heel seat: Check for damage to heel seat. Repair if necessary before proceeding.
3 Select matching heel: Choose heel that matches original height and style as closely as possible.
4 Shape new heel: Use rasp to shape heel to fit perfectly. This is critical for proper fit.
5 Attach heel: Nail or screw heel in place. Use appropriate length fasteners to avoid penetration through insole.
6 Finish heel: Sand smooth, apply edge dressing, and attach heel lift.
⚠️ Warning: Always check for metal shanks before nailing. Hitting a shank can damage tools and the shoe.

Hand Stitching Basics

Medium Difficulty Watch Video Tutorial

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Curved or straight needles
  • Waxed thread (appropriate weight)
  • Awl
  • Pliers (for pulling needles)
  • Thimble
  • Beeswax (for thread)
1 Prepare thread: Cut thread 3-4 times the length of seam. Wax thoroughly for strength and easier handling.
2 Pre-punch holes: Use awl to create holes along stitch line. This protects the leather and ensures straight stitching.
3 Lock stitch technique: Pass needle through hole, then back through same hole to lock. Continue with regular stitching.
4 Maintain tension: Pull each stitch snug but not too tight. Consistent tension creates professional appearance.
5 Finish securely: Backstitch 3-4 times at the end. Knot and trim thread close to surface.
💡 Pro Tip: For leather that's difficult to penetrate, heat your awl slightly with a lighter. This helps pierce tough materials more easily.

Welt Stitching Repair

Hard Watch Video Tutorial

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Curved needle
  • Heavy waxed thread
  • Awl
  • Lasting pliers
  • Shoe last (for support)
1 Access the welt: May need to remove sole or heel to access damaged welt stitching.
2 Remove damaged section: Cut away damaged thread carefully. Note original stitch pattern.
3 Mount on last: Place shoe on appropriate last for support during stitching.
4 Stitch welt: Using curved needle, replicate original stitch pattern. This typically involves stitching through upper, insole, and welt.
5 Reinforce corners: Add extra stitches at heel and toe areas where stress is greatest.
⚠️ Warning: Welt stitching is structural. Poor repair can lead to complete sole separation. If unsure, refer to an experienced cobbler.

Easy

Squeaky Shoes - Common Causes & Fixes

Easy

Problem: Shoes make squeaking noise when walking.

1 Identify the source: Walk slowly and listen carefully. Squeak usually comes from: sole separation, insole rubbing, or leather parts rubbing together.
2 Insole squeak fix: Remove insole, sprinkle baby powder or cornstarch underneath, and replace. This reduces friction.
3 Sole separation squeak: Apply super glue or shoe adhesive to the separated area. Clamp and let dry 24 hours.
4 Leather rubbing squeak: Apply leather conditioner to areas where leather parts meet. This softens and lubricates.
5 New shoe squeak: Often goes away with wear. If not, apply saddle soap to the sole to soften it.

Slipping Heels

Easy

Problem: Heel slips out of shoe when walking.

1 Tongue pad installation: Add cushioned tongue pads to push foot back into heel cup.
2 Heel grips: Stick adhesive heel grips inside back of shoe. These prevent slipping and add comfort.
3 Insole addition: Add thin insole to reduce interior volume and improve fit.
4 Lacing adjustment: For lace-up shoes, try different lacing patterns to lock heel in place.
💡 Pro Tip: Combine heel grips with tongue pads for maximum effectiveness on loose-fitting shoes.

Stiff New Leather

Easy

Problem: New leather shoes are uncomfortable and stiff.

1 Leather conditioner: Apply generous amount of leather conditioner to stiff areas. Let absorb overnight.
2 Wear with thick socks: Wear shoes around the house with thick socks to gradually stretch and soften.
3 Flexing exercise: Manually flex the shoe back and forth repeatedly to break in the sole and upper.
4 Spot treatment: For particularly stiff spots, apply leather softener or mink oil directly to problem areas.

Scuff Marks on Leather

Easy

Problem: Surface scuff marks on smooth leather.

1 Light scuffs: Rub gently with a clean, damp cloth. Many light scuffs will disappear.
2 Pencil eraser method: Use clean white eraser to gently rub scuff marks. Works surprisingly well.
3 Petroleum jelly: Apply tiny amount to scuff, rub in with finger, then buff with soft cloth.
4 Polish touch-up: Apply matching color shoe polish to cover remaining marks. Buff to shine.

Leather Types Identification

Full-Grain Leather: Highest quality. Shows natural grain and markings. Ages beautifully. Used in premium dress shoes and boots. Breathes well and very durable.
Top-Grain Leather: Second-highest quality. Surface lightly sanded. More uniform appearance. Common in quality shoes. Good durability and appearance.
Suede: Underside of leather with napped finish. Soft and flexible. Requires special care. Cannot use regular leather products on it.
Nubuck: Top-grain leather buffed to create nap. More durable than suede. Similar care requirements. Soft velvety texture.
Patent Leather: Leather with high-gloss coating. Very shiny finish. Clean with damp cloth only. Don't use regular leather cleaners.
Bonded Leather: Leather scraps bonded together. Lower quality. Less durable. Common in budget shoes. Limited repair options.
💡 Pro Tip: Water drop test - quality leather absorbs water slowly and darkens. Bonded leather or synthetics won't absorb.

Sole Material Guide

Leather Sole: Traditional dress shoe sole. Breathable and comfortable. Wears faster than rubber. Can be resoled multiple times. Slippery when wet.
Rubber Sole: Most common. Very durable and weather-resistant. Good traction. Various hardness levels available. Excellent for work boots and casual shoes.
Crepe Rubber: Natural rubber with rough texture. Very comfortable. Good shock absorption. Used in casual shoes. Less durable than synthetic rubber.
Polyurethane (PU): Lightweight synthetic. Good cushioning. Not resoleable once worn. Common in athletic and casual shoes. Breaks down over time even without wear.
EVA (Ethylene Vinyl Acetate): Very lightweight foam. Excellent cushioning. Used in athletic shoes. Not repairable when damaged.
Vibram: Brand name for high-quality rubber soles. Excellent durability and traction. Premium replacement option. Various tread patterns available.
💡 Pro Tip: Match sole material to use case: leather for dress, rubber for everyday, specialized rubber for work boots.

Adhesive Compatibility Chart

Contact Cement: Best for: sole attachment, heel attachment. Works on: leather to rubber, rubber to rubber. Application: apply to both surfaces, wait until tacky, press together firmly.
Shoe Goo: Best for: sole repairs, hole patches, rubber reinforcement. Works on: rubber, leather, canvas. Application: apply directly, smooth, let cure 24 hours.
Barge Cement: Best for: professional sole work, heavy repairs. Works on: most materials. Application: similar to contact cement but stronger bond. Industry standard.
E6000: Best for: hardware attachment, small repairs. Works on: metal, plastic, leather, rubber. Application: apply, clamp, cure 24-72 hours for full strength.
Leather Glue: Best for: leather tears, patch work. Works on: leather to leather only. Application: apply thin layer, press, clamp if possible.
Super Glue (Cyanoacrylate): Best for: emergency repairs, small parts. Works on: most non-porous materials. Application: very small amount, instant bond, not flexible.
⚠️ Warning: Never mix different adhesive types. Always allow one to fully cure before applying another.

Thread Selection Guide

Waxed Polyester (Size 138): General purpose hand stitching. Good for most upper repairs. Strong and weather resistant.
Waxed Linen: Traditional choice for quality work. Excellent for visible stitching. Ages well. Used in high-end repairs.
Nylon (Size 69): Machine stitching uppers. Good for canvas and fabric. Strong and stretchy. Not ideal for leather hand stitching.
Heavy Waxed (Size 207+): Welt stitching and sole work. Extra strength for structural repairs. Requires larger needles.
💡 Pro Tip: Always wax your thread even if it's pre-waxed. Extra wax makes stitching easier and creates stronger, more water-resistant seams.

Workshop Safety Essentials

⚠️ Chemical Safety:
  • Always work in well-ventilated area when using adhesives
  • Wear nitrile gloves when handling chemicals
  • Keep adhesives away from heat sources and open flames
  • Store chemicals in original containers with labels
  • Have eyewash station or running water available
  • Never eat or drink in work area
⚠️ Tool Safety:
  • Keep all cutting tools sharp - dull tools require more force and are more dangerous
  • Always cut away from your body
  • Use cutting mat or appropriate work surface
  • Wear safety glasses when using rotary tools or sanders
  • Keep fingers clear of needle path when stitching
  • Unplug machines when changing parts or cleaning
💡 First Aid Basics:
  • Keep first aid kit stocked: bandages, antiseptic, burn cream
  • For cuts: apply pressure, clean, bandage. Seek medical care for deep cuts
  • For chemical exposure: flush with water for 15 minutes minimum
  • For glue on skin: don't pull apart - soak in warm soapy water

Ventilation Requirements

Contact Cement & Strong Adhesives: Requires excellent ventilation. Open windows and use fans. Consider respirator for extended use. Fumes can cause headaches and dizziness.
Dyes and Finishes: Moderate ventilation needed. Work near open window. Fumes can irritate eyes and respiratory system.
Leather Cleaners: Generally safe but ventilate area. Some cleaners have strong odors.
Signs of Poor Ventilation: Headache, dizziness, nausea, eye irritation. Stop work immediately and get fresh air if experiencing these symptoms.
⚠️ Warning: Some people are more sensitive to fumes. If you feel unwell, stop working and ventilate area thoroughly.

Proper Lifting and Ergonomics

Workbench Height: Should be at comfortable standing height (typically waist level). Prevents back strain during detailed work.
Seating: Use adjustable stool or chair for sitting work. Maintain good posture. Take breaks every hour.
Hand Position: Keep wrists neutral when stitching or cutting. Avoid repetitive motions without breaks.
Heavy Items: Lift with legs, not back. Store heavy items at waist level when possible.
💡 Pro Tip: Stretching hands and wrists regularly prevents repetitive strain injuries common in repair work.

Fire Safety

Fire Extinguisher: Keep ABC-rated fire extinguisher accessible. Know how to use it. Check pressure gauge monthly.
Flammable Materials: Store adhesives in metal cabinet away from heat sources. Keep only needed amounts in work area.
Electrical Safety: Don't overload outlets. Check cords for damage. Keep electrical equipment away from water.
Waste Disposal: Dispose of solvent-soaked rags in metal container with lid. Empty regularly.
⚠️ Warning: Solvent-soaked rags can spontaneously combust. Never leave in regular trash or piled in workshop.

Leather Cleaning and Conditioning

Easy

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Soft brushes
  • Leather cleaner
  • Leather conditioner
  • Soft cloths
  • Saddle soap (for deep cleaning)
1 Remove loose dirt: Brush off surface dirt with soft brush. Remove laces and clean separately.
2 Clean leather: Apply leather cleaner with damp cloth. Work in small sections using circular motions.
3 Dry completely: Allow shoes to air dry naturally. Never use direct heat sources.
4 Condition: Apply leather conditioner evenly. Let absorb for 20-30 minutes.
5 Buff and polish: Buff with clean cloth. Apply polish if desired for shine.
💡 Pro Tip: Test any cleaner or conditioner on a hidden area first to ensure color compatibility.

Suede and Nubuck Care

Easy

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • Suede brush
  • Suede eraser
  • White vinegar (for stains)
  • Suede protector spray
  • Soft cloth
1 Brush gently: Use suede brush in one direction to lift nap and remove surface dirt.
2 Treat stains: Use suede eraser for scuffs. For water stains, dampen entire area evenly then let dry.
3 Remove tough stains: Lightly dampen cloth with white vinegar and blot stained area. Let dry completely.
4 Restore nap: Once dry, brush vigorously to restore texture.
5 Protect: Apply suede protector spray to guard against future stains.
⚠️ Warning: Never use regular leather cleaners or conditioners on suede. They will damage the napped surface.

Salt Stain Removal

Easy

🛠️ Tools Needed:

  • White vinegar
  • Water
  • Soft cloth or sponge
  • Leather conditioner
1 Mix solution: Mix equal parts white vinegar and water in small bowl.
2 Apply to stains: Dampen cloth with solution and gently wipe salt-stained areas.
